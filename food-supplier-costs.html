Reduce Your Food & Supplier Costs

Food is the single biggest controllable expense in a restaurant — typically 28–35% of sales. Most operators negotiate it once, then let prices creep for years. We put your supplier spend back out to bid and keep it honest.

The math that matters: a restaurant doing $1M a year at a 33% food cost spends $330,000 on product. Cutting that by 2 points — very achievable with bid-outs and invoice auditing — returns $20,000 a year without changing a single recipe.

Where supplier money leaks

  • Price creep. Broadliners commonly raise prices on your top items a few cents at a time between contract reviews. Without line-item auditing, nobody notices.
  • Single-vendor loyalty. Convenient, but the moment a distributor knows you don't compare, your pricing reflects it.
  • No contract at all. Many independents buy at street price when their volume qualifies them for fixed-markup agreements.
  • Buying alone. Group purchasing organizations (GPOs) pool thousands of restaurants to get chain-level pricing — most independents have never joined one.
  • Menu blind spots. A handful of items usually drive most of your food cost. Without menu engineering, low-margin items stay front and center.

How we cut your supplier costs

1. Top-20 item bid-out

Roughly 20 SKUs typically represent the bulk of your food spend. We build a clean bid sheet from your purchase history and put those items out to competing distributors. You keep your preferred vendor relationships — with better numbers.

2. Group purchasing power

Through partner GPOs, independent restaurants access manufacturer deviations and contract pricing normally reserved for chains. Joining is typically free; savings show up on the same trucks from the same distributors.

3. Invoice price-creep auditing

We benchmark your invoices monthly against agreed pricing and market indexes, and flag every unauthorized increase. Recovered credits are common in the first 90 days.

4. Fixed-markup agreements

Where volume justifies it, we negotiate cost-plus contracts so your distributor earns a transparent markup instead of whatever the street will bear.

5. Menu and yield quick wins

We pair purchasing data with menu engineering basics — portion specs, yield tests, and highlighting high-margin items — so savings compound on the menu side too.

What to gather before we talk

  1. Your last 4 weeks of supplier invoices (or a purchase history export from your distributor portal)
  2. Current contract or markup agreement, if any
  3. Your menu with current prices

Food & supplier cost FAQs

What should my food cost percentage be?

Most full-service restaurants target 28–35% of sales; quick service often runs lower, steakhouses higher. The better benchmark is prime cost (food + labor), which healthy restaurants keep at or below 60–65% of sales.

Will bidding out my order damage my distributor relationship?

No — it professionalizes it. Distributors expect serious operators to compare. Most incumbent vendors sharpen pricing to keep the business rather than lose it.

What is a GPO and what does it cost?

A group purchasing organization pools many restaurants' volume to negotiate manufacturer-level pricing. Most restaurant GPOs are free to join — they're funded by manufacturer fees — and work through the distributors you already use.

How fast do supplier savings show up?

Bid-out and GPO savings typically land within one to two order cycles. Invoice audit recoveries begin with the first month's review.
